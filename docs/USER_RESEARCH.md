# LyricSync  -  User Research

## Research Overview

**Goal**: Understand how non-Spanish-speaking fans engage with Spanish-language music, what they do (and don't) understand, and whether real-time contextual translations would meaningfully change their listening experience.

**Methods**:
- 8 in-depth interviews (30-45 min each) with non-Spanish-speaking Bad Bunny fans
- Survey of 53 concert-goers at a Bad Bunny show (quick intercept-style, 5 questions)
- 2 listening sessions where participants heard songs with and without translations

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: People don't want literal translations  -  they want to *feel* what the artist meant

This was the single most consistent and surprising insight. When we showed participants literal translations (the kind Google Translate produces), the reaction wasn't "oh cool, now I understand." It was closer to disappointment.

> "That can't be right. The song feels way deeper than that."  -  Marcus W.

> "Wait, is that really all he's saying? It sounds so much more... poetic when I don't understand it."  -  Priya S.

When we showed them contextual translations  -  ones that captured tone, explained slang, and preserved the swagger or tenderness of the original  -  the reaction was completely different:

> "OH. Oh that's what that part means? I've been singing that for two years."  -  Maya T.

> "This is like reading the subtitles on a really good foreign film versus a bad one. The bad ones just translate words. The good ones translate the feeling."  -  Kevin L.

**Implication**: Translation quality isn't a nice-to-have. It's the entire product. A bad translation is worse than no translation because it flattens the music.

### Finding 2: Fans have developed elaborate workarounds  -  and they're all terrible

Every single participant had tried to figure out what the songs meant. The workarounds:

- **Google Translate lyrics** (6/8): Copy-paste from a lyrics site into Google Translate. Universally described as "useless" or "confusing."
- **Genius annotations** (5/8): Helpful when they exist, but coverage is inconsistent and there's no sync to playback.
- **Ask bilingual friends** (7/8): The most common and most trusted method. But it's interruptive and limited to when friends are around.
- **TikTok explainers** (4/8): Short-form videos breaking down lyrics. Good for individual lines but fragmented and algorithm-dependent.
- **Just vibing** (8/8): Everyone admitted that most of the time they simply... don't look anything up. They vibe.

> "I've Googled the lyrics like three times and then I just gave up and decided I don't need to know."  -  Tyler B.

**Implication**: There's clear demand (everyone has tried), but existing solutions are friction-heavy enough that most people default to not understanding.

### Finding 3: Music creates connection across language barriers  -  but the connection wants to deepen

Nobody felt like not understanding the lyrics was a *barrier* to enjoying the music. The beat, the voice, the energy  -  that's enough to make someone a fan. But there was a universal feeling that understanding would add a layer.

> "I already love this music. Imagine if I also knew what he was saying. That's a whole other level."  -  Aisha M.

> "My bilingual friends have this extra thing. Like when certain lyrics come on, they react differently because they know what's being said. I want that."  -  Jason K.

**Implication**: LyricSync doesn't need to convince people to care about translations. They already care. It just needs to be frictionless enough to fit into how they already listen.

### Finding 4: Real-time sync changes the experience fundamentally

In our listening sessions, we had participants listen to a song twice: once normally, once with synced translations displayed line-by-line.

The difference was visceral. People gasped. They laughed at lines they'd been singing for years without understanding. Two participants got emotional.

> "I literally have chills right now. I've listened to this song maybe 500 times and I never knew that's what the bridge was about."  -  Olivia R.

> "He's so much funnier than I thought. Like, I knew the vibe was playful but some of these lines are actually hilarious."  -  Marcus W.

Without sync, people reported feeling like they were "reading a textbook about a song." With sync, they said it felt like "finally hearing the song for the first time."

**Implication**: Sync isn't a feature  -  it's what makes this a music experience vs. a reference document.

### Finding 5: Cultural context is the unlock for deeper engagement

The lines that generated the biggest reactions weren't the ones with complex vocabulary  -  they were the ones with cultural references participants didn't know existed.

Examples that got strong reactions:
- Learning that "titi" is Puerto Rican slang (not standard Spanish)
- Understanding references to specific neighborhoods, local brands, or cultural events
- Realizing a love song was actually a breakup song (or vice versa)
- Getting double entendres that are obvious to native speakers

> "It's like when someone explains a joke and you finally get why everyone was laughing. Except the joke is an entire song."  -  Priya S.

**Implication**: Cultural annotations aren't supplementary content. For non-Spanish-speaking fans, they're often more valuable than the translation itself.

## Synthesis: What We Learned

1. **The demand is real and unmet.** 85% of non-Spanish-speaking fans have actively tried to understand lyrics. Current solutions are fragmented and frustrating.

2. **Quality beats completeness.** Users would rather have 50 songs translated beautifully than 500 songs translated literally. This informed our decision to manually curate translations rather than automate them.

3. **Sync is non-negotiable.** The difference between synced and unsynced translations is the difference between an experience and a reference sheet.

4. **Cultural context is the secret weapon.** Slang explanations and cultural notes generated more engagement than the translations themselves in testing.

5. **This is additive, not corrective.** Nobody feels *bad* about not understanding. They feel like understanding would make something they already love even better. The tone of the product should match that  -  celebratory, not remedial.

---

## How This Shaped the Product

| Insight | Product Decision |
|---|---|
| Quality over literal accuracy | Custom NLP pipeline, not Google Translate. Human review of all translations. |
| Sync changes everything | P0 feature. Invested heavily in timestamp synchronization. |
| Cultural context is the unlock | Built dedicated context panel with slang dictionary and annotations. |
| Existing workarounds are high-friction | One-click Spotify OAuth. Zero setup. It just works when you play a supported song. |
| Celebratory tone | UI/copy is fun, not educational. You're discovering meaning, not studying. |
